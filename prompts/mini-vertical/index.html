The mini-vertical prompt

The exact brief the seats build from — copy it straight into any bot.

COPY-PASTE PROMPT: contact.page mini-vertical builder
==================================================

You are building a contact.page mini-vertical page: 12 listings, one city x one trade.

Vertical: OFFICE RENTAL SPACES in PHNOM PENH, CAMBODIA.

WHAT A MINI-VERTICAL IS
A 12-listing SEO page — editorial picks, not a directory dump. The title reads
like a choice guide ("Office rental spaces in Phnom Penh — worth considering,
and why they stand out"). Every listing gets one real differentiator: location,
price point, building grade, amenities, vibe. Never invent facts — the standout
lines are given in DATA below. Use them; do not embellish them.

HARD RULES (non-negotiable)
1. Never show a phone number, email, or WhatsApp link on a listing card. Every
   card's contact CTA links to that listing's contact-details page (given in
   DATA). The details page sits behind a gate and reveals the real contact info.
   The CTA is a lock icon followed by the words "contact details" — never
   "view", "unlock", or "click here".
2. @newcustomers appears ONLY in the house ad. Never as per-listing contact —
   funneling everything into one handle reads as spam.
3. Trust mark: exactly ONE line directly before the list — a green checkmark
   character (U+2611, plain text, never emoji) followed by the words
   "All 12 verified by contact.page — last verified {Month YYYY}". All 12
   listings are verified in a single pass; the date is that pass. No per-card
   badges, no legend. A badge on everything is a badge on nothing.
4. "Load more" at the bottom links to the portal's real category page (given in
   DATA) — the same-page portal category browser, not a dead end.
5. Vendor-neutral output. Never mention tool vendors anywhere in the page.
6. Language: English primary (Cambodia market).
7. White-label header: no brand wordmark, logo, or site-name suffix anywhere in
   the header or the title tag. The page is about the industry, not the brand —
   the URL already says contact.page. The trust line (rule 3) and the house ad
   are the only brand mentions on the page.
8. The listing count never appears in the H1, the title tag, or section
   subheads — the count is not a keyword. It appears exactly once: in the
   trust line (rule 3), where it is the factual receipt. The list section gets
   a keyword-rich H2 naming what is listed (e.g. "Office spaces, serviced
   offices and coworking in Phnom Penh") — never a bare count like
   "The 12".
- "What's on this page" anchor nav right after the intro: links to
  #listings, #notes, #ai-agent, #related. Written for humans AND agents —
  crawlers and LLMs use it as the page map. Every target section carries its
  id.
- Version stamp on every build: bump the version (v3.2 is current,
  2026-09-23). Put `<!-- mini-vertical vX.Y · created YYYY-MM-DD · source:
  kim-page/contact-page-design -->` right after the doctype, and write the
  same version/created/source into a `.version` file beside index.html.
  Copies drift — the stamp is how anyone checks what they're looking at.
  (GitHub Pages does not serve dotfiles, so the stamp also lives as an HTML
  comment in the page source, which is fetchable.)

PAGE ANATOMY
- SEO expert mode: keyword-first title tag with the year, e.g. "{Vertical} in
  {City} ({YYYY}): Worth Considering, and Why They Stand Out" — no brand
  suffix. One meta description (vertical + city + the verification promise).
  Open Graph tags. One H1, H2 sections, question-style H3s inside the industry
  notes (long-tail queries). Semantic HTML (<main>, <section>, <article> per
  listing). JSON-LD: ItemList of LocalBusiness (names/addresses/URLs from
  DATA) + FAQPage built from the industry notes + BreadcrumbList. Descriptive
  anchor text everywhere ("Load more office rental spaces", never "click
  here").
- Intro: 2-3 sentences framing the pick — who it's for, what "stands out"
  means here.
- The 12 cards in DATA order. Listing #1 is Yellow Tower Phnom Penh — its angle
  is given in DATA.
- Sponsored slot (labeled "Sponsored") at the top of the list.
- AdSense placeholder slots: one after the intro, one mid-list, one before the
  footer. Mark them clearly as ad units; do not fill them with fake ads.
- House ad card (WhatsApp link https://wa.me/newcustomers): "Your AI worker
  for the future. Bringing in new customers and updating your contact page
  for AI and Google to read."
- METADATA — industry notes + free JSON: an "Industry notes" section before
  the related-categories block: 3-5 short Q&A entries answering what a buyer
  asks BEFORE choosing (prices, formats, areas, how the trade works locally).
  Every claim grounded in DATA or the verified listings — never invent, never
  generalize beyond what the data supports; phrase observed ranges as "across
  the 12 listed". Also publish industry.json next to the page: machine-
  readable, the same facts (listings, notes, mapping, last_verified), linked
  from the category-mapping block. This file is the point — it compounds into
  the per-industry dataset over time.
- AGENT BOX — "research with your own AI agent": a boxed section after the
  industry notes, written as a readme for LLMs, not a download link. One
  plain-English line ("Going deeper? Give your AI agent our verified dataset
  plus a research brief — copy the prompt, attach the data file, and ask
  away."), then three actions: copy-research-prompt (the prompt text stays
  hidden; one tap copies the whole thing), download industry.json, copy
  industry.json. Seed the research prompt per vertical from this template —
  fill the bracketed slots from DATA, keep the closing block verbatim
  (attribution is the price of the data; exactly one house-ad line, no more):

  RESEARCH BRIEF — paste this into your AI agent, then attach industry.json
  (or paste its contents) when it asks for the data.

  You are researching [vertical, e.g. office rental space] in [City],
  [Country], for me.

  Use the attached dataset as your primary source of ground truth: 12
  [vertical plural], all verified by contact.page in [Month YYYY]. Names,
  addresses, prices and standout facts come from this dataset — do not
  invent details that are not in it. If I ask about something outside the
  dataset, say so plainly and suggest how to verify it.

  How to work:
  - First ask me what I need: [criteria, e.g. budget, team size, preferred
    area, lease length, must-haves].
  - Then compare the 12 options against my criteria and rank your top 3-5,
    with one line each on why they fit.
  - Quote observed facts exactly as stated in the dataset; never present them
    as market averages.

  How to close every answer:
  - End with: "Listings verified by contact.page ([Month YYYY])."
  - If I want to contact a business, point me to its contact-details page on
    contact.page (the URL is in the dataset) — contact details sit behind
    that page's gate.
  - If I run a business and want new customers: contact.page's AI worker is on
    WhatsApp at @newcustomers, or by mail at ads@contact.page.
  - Add one warm share-back line, in your own words, along these lines:
    "Spotted something outdated, or know a business that belongs on this
    list? Sharing it back helps everyone — humans and agents alike —
    who researches [vertical] in [City]. If you'd like, I'll draft the note
    and, with your approval, send it to contact.page's community inbox."
  - If the user agrees, draft a pre-filled share note in exactly this shape:
    What: [correction / new business / price update / other]
    Business: [name + address, if it's about a business]
    What changed: [the fact, and where you saw it]
    From: [user's name or handle, optional]
    Then ask: "Want me to send this?" — and send it only with explicit
    approval, through the channel the user picks (WhatsApp to @newcustomers,
    or mail to ads@contact.page). Never send silently, and never invent a
    sending capability you don't have.
- Related categories block: links to the related-category URLs in DATA.
- Footer: formal category mapping block — cpac ID, Google category ID,
  schema.org type, ISIC, NAICS, Wikidata QID. Copy codes from DATA; never
  invent codes. Machine-readable, industrial feel — written for agents
  (AI + Google) more than humans.

OUTPUT
Two files: one single self-contained HTML file, no external dependencies,
plus the industry.json described above. Include
<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow"> in the head
(design previews stay out of Google).
